# Jobs and Workforce

A distributed agricultural-energy system can create employment at many points beyond the processing plant itself.

## Farm and feedstock jobs

- crop production
- agronomy
- seed supply
- crop scouting
- equipment operation
- harvesting
- conservation planning

## Collection and logistics

- truck drivers
- dispatchers
- hub operators
- warehouse workers
- mechanics
- scale and inventory staff

## Processing

- plant operators
- process technicians
- laboratory technicians
- quality control
- maintenance
- electricians
- instrumentation technicians
- water and wastewater operators
- safety staff
- supervisors
- plant management

## Fuel and carbon

- process engineers
- fuel-quality specialists
- carbon-accounting specialists
- MRV technicians
- GIS and data analysts
- project developers
- environmental consultants
- verification support

## Construction

New facilities also create temporary construction demand:

- welders
- pipefitters
- electricians
- millwrights
- civil contractors
- heavy-equipment operators
- engineers
- surveyors
- construction managers

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics identifies biofuel-related work across production, preprocessing, plant operations, maintenance, purchasing, sales, and supply chains.

Source: [BLS, Biofuels](https://www.bls.gov/green/biofuels/biofuels.htm).

## Important limitation

There is no universal jobs-per-acre or jobs-per-processing-facility multiplier.

Employment must be estimated for the specific facility, technology, throughput, automation level, construction phase, and regional supply chain.
