# Winter Camelina

Winter camelina is one of the proposed oilseed feedstocks for the CarbonShare/SAF system.

## Why camelina?

USDA-ARS research describes winter camelina as a potential biofuel feedstock that can be integrated into Midwestern cropping systems.

USDA research has reported winter-camelina yields around **1,100–1,300 kg/ha in Minnesota under good stands** in relevant research contexts.

Source: [USDA-ARS research](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/project?accnNo=437622&fy=2025).

University of Minnesota Extension has also described winter camelina as an oilseed crop that can be used in relay or double-cropping systems with soybeans.

Source: [University of Minnesota Extension, Winter Camelina](https://blog-crop-news.extension.umn.edu/2024/10/winter-camelina-new-oilseed-crop-for.html).

## What still needs to be demonstrated

The crop's existence does not establish project economics.

The pilot must evaluate:

- yield by location and year;
- planting and harvest timing;
- oil content;
- storage;
- collection cost;
- crushing/extraction;
- meal value;
- contracts;
- crop-management risk;
- lifecycle emissions;
- SAF pathway qualification;
- farmer return.
