# SAF Feedstock Economics

The existence of a SAF market does not by itself establish that a particular crop is profitable.

The economic chain must be modeled from farm to final fuel.

## Variables

**Farm**

- yield
- input costs
- crop insurance
- planting/harvest costs
- opportunity cost
- rotation effects

**Aggregation**

- storage
- drying
- trucking
- handling
- hub operating costs

**Processing**

- oil yield
- meal yield
- energy use
- capital cost
- labor
- maintenance

**Downstream**

- fuel pathway
- conversion yield
- certification
- transportation
- blending
- market price

## Camelina

A 2025 peer-reviewed study modeled camelina, pennycress, and carinata as potential SAF feedstocks and found that meal credits could materially affect modeled bio-oil costs.

Source: [Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S136403212500228X).

These results are scenario-dependent. OurTown should use them as evidence for what must be modeled, not as a guaranteed Minnesota project return.
